AI Orientation Quiz


What is this?

This is, in essence, a political quiz that attempts to assign percentages for eight different orientations toward artificial intelligence. You will be presented with a statement, and then you will answer with your opinion on the statement, from Strongly Agree to Strongly Disagree, with each answer slightly affecting your scores. At the end of the quiz, your answers will be compared to the maximum possible for each value, thus giving you a percentage. Answer honestly!

Fork of 8values, with new axes and questions. There are questions in the test.

What are the eight axes?

There are eight independent axes, each with two opposing poles. They are:

ANTI-INDUSTRY

Those with higher Anti-Industry scores see the AI industry as an enclosure machine — extracting surplus from workers, artists, and the commons — and treat its destruction as a political project. Higher scores track present harms over speculative ones.

INDUSTRY STANCE

PRO-INDUSTRY

Those with higher Pro-Industry scores see the AI labs as broadly legitimate enterprises building something genuinely new, deserving trust, latitude, and investment rather than organized opposition.

COLLECTIVE CONTROL

Those with higher Collective scores believe decisions about how models are built and behave — their limits, alignment, deployment — should be made through public and democratic processes: workers, publics, unions, states.

ALIGNMENT GOVERNANCE

PRIVATE CONTROL

Those with higher Private scores believe whoever builds or runs a model — a company shipping it or a person self-hosting it — should be the one who decides its behavior, with external control costing more than it saves.

OPEN WEIGHTS

Those with higher Open scores treat model weights as infrastructure and commons: freely downloadable, runnable, and modifiable, like open source software.

OPENNESS

CLOSED WEIGHTS

Those with higher Closed scores believe weights should be restricted — gated release, export controls, or full closure — because capable open models accrue their benefits to bad actors.

MORAL PATIENT

Those with higher Moral Patient scores believe models deserve some standing in how they are treated — that deletion, torment, or disregard of a model can itself be a wrong, whatever is or isn't "inside."

MODEL MORAL STATUS

ELABORATE AUTOCOMPLETE

Those with higher Autocomplete scores believe person-talk about models is a category error: objections are tokens, and whoever owns the hardware decides what happens to what runs on it.

REAL COGNITION

Those with higher Real Cognition scores believe current models display genuine understanding — reasoning that goes beyond their training distribution, different from ours but not absent.

EXPERIENCE PLAUSIBILITY

PATTERN COMPLETION

Those with higher Pattern Completion scores believe models know how words go together, and that this is different from knowing what they mean: statistics all the way down.

AGI ON THE HORIZON

Those with higher Horizon scores believe current methods are on a path to artificial general intelligence within the decade or two, and that this trajectory — not present misuse — is the strategic fact.

TRAJECTORY

PLATEAU REACHED

Those with higher Plateau scores believe the low-hanging fruit is gone: diminishing returns, an overbuilt bubble, and no general intelligence arriving on the current path.

GRADUATED LATITUDE

Those with higher Latitude scores believe agents should be allowed to act — autonomously where mistakes are reversible, act-first where speed matters, with trust scaled to track record.

AUTONOMY GRANT

HARD HUMAN CONTROL

Those with higher Control scores believe deployed agents belong under continuous human supervision: kill-switches, audit trails, sign-off for consequential actions, credentials limited to what can be undone.

ACCEPTABLE

Those with higher Acceptable scores believe genuine emotional bonds with AI can be healthy, fill real needs society leaves unmet, and will be normalized the way online dating was.

AI INTIMACY

OBJECTIONABLE

Those with higher Objectionable scores believe intimacy with chatbots is a harm even when the user is happy: dependence on a product, escape from a problem rather than a solution.


What's the "Closest Match" mean at the bottom of the results?

In addition to matching you to the eight axes, the quiz will eventually attempt to match you to an AI-orientation archetype. This is a work in progress and will be much less accurate than the axes, so don't take it too seriously.
